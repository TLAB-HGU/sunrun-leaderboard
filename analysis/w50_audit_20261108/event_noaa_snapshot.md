Solar Activity

.24 hr Summary...
Solar activity was at low levels with only smaller than C5 flares
throughout the reporting period. Region 4341 (S11W09, Eko/beta)
continued to show magnetic consolidation after its X1.9 flare on 18/1809
UTC. Regions 4342 (N14W04, Dsi/beta-gamma) and 4345 (S17W02,
Dai/beta-gamma) showed significant flux emergence throughout the period,
with their
intermediary areas displaying very mixed polarities. All other regions
were either stable or in decay.

Coronagraph imagery continued to be contaminated by signatures of solar
energetic particles, complicating detection of potential eruptions.
However, both later coronagraph and disk imagery indicate no potentially
Earth-directed CMEs during the reporting period.

.Forecast...
Solar activity is likely (60%) to be at moderate (R1/R2, Minor/Moderate)
levels, with a slight chance (10%) for X-class flares (R3-Strong) on
20-22 Jan, primarily due to the magnetic potential of Regions 4341,
4342, and 4345.

Energetic Particle

.24 hr Summary...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux reached high levels with a peak
flux of 7,880 pfu at 19/1720 UTC, followed by a sharp decrease below the
1,000 pfu threshold around 19/2000 UTC, likely associated with the Halo
CME arrival near Earth. 

The greater than 10 MeV proton flux crossed the S4 (Severe) storming
levels at 19/1810 UTC, reaching a maximum flux of 37,000 pfu at 19/1915
UTC. This proton flux was the highest observed since Mar 1991 and likely
reflected the particle acceleration caused by the X1.9 flare and/or Halo
CME shock. The flux dropped below S3 (Strong) levels around 19/2130 UTC
and below S2 (Moderate) around 20/0900.

.Forecast...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux is expected to continue reaching
high levels through 22 Jan. The greater than 10 MeV proton flux is
expected to decrease from Strong to Minor storming levels (S3 to S1)
on Jan 20 and gradually drop below the S1 threshold on 21-22 Jan.

Solar Wind

.24 hr Summary...
Solar wind parameters reflected positive polarity CH influences until
19/1902 UTC, when a Sudden Impulse was observed, indicating the initial
arrival of the Halo CMEs interplanetary shock near Earth. The total
magnetic field strength (Bt) reached a maximum of 91 nT at 19/2115 UTC,
dropped to an average about 65 nT at 19/2200 UTC, and then began a
steady
decay at 20/0000 UTC, ending the reporting period at 14 nT. The Bz
(north-south) component initially deflected south/negative, reaching a
max of -58 nT at 19/2104 UTC before immediately going north/positive.
Around 20/0500 UTC, Bz deflected back southward and at 20/0557 UTC began
maintaining an average of approximately -20 nT until 20/0945 UTC when Bz
slowly began deflecting back northward; Bz ended the reporting period at
-5 nT.

For the first half of the reporting period, the solar wind speed
observed by operational aging assets at L1 (ACE and DSCOVR) were
unphysical as they were most likely being compromised by the extremely
strong on-going proton event. During this interval, preliminary
(non-operational) SOLAR-1 (SWFO-L1) new L1 in-situ data indicated that
the solar wind speed reached 942 km/s at 19/2052 UTC during shock
arrival. ACE and DSCOVR data became reliable once again at 19/2115 UTC,
showing a wind speed of about 1135 km/s that decreased slowly to about
900-950 km/s at 20/0315 UTC, which was sustained until the end of the
reporting period.

Phi angle was oriented in the positive solar sector while the +CH HSS
influence was dominant, and oscillated between positive and negative
sector during the CME arrival/passage influences (after about 19/1900
UTC), before returning to the positive sector after 19/2355 UTC.

.Forecast...
Enhanced solar wind conditions are expected to persist through 20 Jan
due to the combined influences from the recurrent, positive polarity
coronal hole and complete passage of the halo CME that left the Sun on
18 Jan. CME influences near Earth will likely diminish between 21-22
Jan, thus the solar wind parameters are expected to reflect +CH HSS
conditions by the end of 22 Jan.

Geospace

.24 hr Summary...
Geomagnetic activity levels ranged from quiet to Severe (G4) storming
levels, due to combined influences of +CH HSS and a Halo CME arrival.
The GOES-R satellites magnetometer observed a dramatic drop on the Hp
component, suggesting a strong compression of the magnetopause, possibly
crossing over geostationary orbit.

.Forecast...
The geomagnetic activity is expected to continue reaching Minor to
Severe (G1 to G4) storming levels on 20 Jan, as the combined influences
of +CH HSS and the Halo CME passage persists. Active to Minor storming
(G1) levels are likely on 21 Jan as CME effects wane. Unsettled to
Active levels are anticipated on 22 Jan as influence from the positive
polarity CH HSS resumes dominating the near-Earth environment.
