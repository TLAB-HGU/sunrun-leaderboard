# Research: observation validity and physical forecast limits

Retrieved2026-11-08 via Exa search/fetch and GitHub read-only tools. Comprehensive but bounded research; no models fitted and no benchmark policy changed. Local findings below are supplied by the audit team, not independently re-inspected in this lane.

## Direct answer

The strongest new evidence is an official NOAA bulletin documenting both compromised ACE/DSCOVR wind-speed observations during the January19,2026 proton storm **and their subsequent recovery**. The leader's residual audit identifies leading error targets at January19 22:00=1080.3,23:00=1044.3 and January20 00:00=1035.6km/s: all are after NOAA's reported19/21:15 recovery. These top points cannot be blamed on the earlier contamination; their magnitude is consistent with NOAA's approximately1135km/s then900–950km/s description. Exact independent Level2/Wind/DSCOVR corroboration remains unresolved. A real extreme CME occurred, so the bulletin does not license rejecting the whole event. No paper establishes that the present official S can be halved.

## Official evidence and its limits

1. [NOAA forecast discussion, issued2026-01-20 13:35UTC](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/stp/space-weather/swpc-products/daily_reports/discussion/2026/01/202601201230forecast_discussion.txt) explicitly states: “For the first half of the reporting period, the solar wind speed observed by operational aging assets at L1 (ACE and DSCOVR) were unphysical as they were most likely being compromised by the extremely strong on-going proton event.” It says they became reliable again at January19 21:15UTC, with speed about1135km/s, then900–950km/s from January20 03:15. Preliminary non-operational SOLAR-1 measured942km/s at January19 20:52 during shock arrival. The bulletin also records a halo-CME shock/sudden impulse near19:02, proton flux crossing S4 at18:10 and peaking37000pfu at19:15. These are directly documented facts; the precise contamination of each local hourly target remains to be checked.
2. [NOAA S4 announcement,2026-01-19 18:54UTC](https://www.spaceweather.gov/news/s4-severe-solar-radiation-storm-progress-january-19th-2026) independently confirms the severe proton event. It does not quantify hourly solar-wind measurement error.
3. [SWPC RTSW README](https://sohoftp.nascom.nasa.gov/sdb/goes/ace/monthly/README), dated2010-12-27, calls its archive preliminary reports and discourages their use as final scientific data; completed lists are not altered. It defines status0 nominal,1–8 bad,9 no received data, and :10 hourly updates. A nominal flag is a product screening rule, not proof of error-free speed under every event. This old operational specification is useful but cannot alone establish all2026 release vintages or explain the observed six-hour clock-dependent gaps.
4. [ACE SWEPAM Level2 description](https://izw1.caltech.edu/ACE/ASC/level2/swepam_l2desc.html), updated October2025, and [instrument-team release notes](https://izw1.caltech.edu/ACE/ASC/DATA/level2/swepam/swepam_release_notes) document hand-checked moments, detector-gain reprocessing, calibration/pointing effects and penetrating-particle contamination. The2021 release reprocessed2013 onward and particularly improved density; older notes say speed may remain valid when other moments fail. Historical validity percentages and2002 speed changes<0.5% are **not** estimates of2026 error or a present noise floor. Retrieve the applicable version and same-spacecraft time interval before numerical comparison.
5. [NASA high-resolution OMNI documentation](https://omniweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/html/HROdocum.html) states record time tags are target-arrival times at the bow-shock nose, not observation times at ACE. It uses convecting phase fronts and intersperses spacecraft. The documentation warns that shifting can alternate pre/post-shock domains and smear a clean shock over an interval. Therefore direct timestamp subtraction of OMNI and ACE is not an instrument-error estimate. Keep official ACE scoring unchanged; use spacecraft identity, time-shift, interpolation and averaging metadata in a separate audit.

## Primary literature: applicable mechanisms, incompatible score claims

- [Rotter et al.2015](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.06697): AIA193Å coronal-hole area and ACE Level2,2011–2013, approximately4day forecast. Reported transport delay4.02±0.5days, HSS peak correlation~0.60,80% peak arrivals within±0.5days. This supports distinct timing and amplitude diagnostics. It does not prove all remaining errors are timing errors or specify a lower bound on1–72h official S.
- [Collin et al.2025, DOI10.1029/2024SW004125](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024SW004125):4day AIA/OMNI prediction, spatial coronal-hole geometry/evolution,26/27/28day solar-wind history and solar-cycle features. It reports timeline RMSE68.1km/s for2010–2019 and80.3km/s for2020–2023; **CME intervals are removed** (84024→45933 and35064→21064 points). These numbers are not a like-for-like SOTA comparison with this repository. MSE peak attenuation and optional distribution transformation are mechanisms to investigate, not permission to calibrate against F1/F2/F3 truth.
- [NASA CCMC SIML-HSS registry](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/models/SIML-HSS~1/) confirms the geometry+recurrence inputs and explicitly excludes CME prediction. Coronal-hole information alone cannot be presumed sufficient for transient CME extremes.

## Evidence boundary and next decision

Confirmed externally: energetic-particle contamination is a real instrument mechanism; NOAA specifically reported compromised L1 speed followed by recovery during the Jan19 event; HRO timing differs from ACE; CH/HSS and CME mechanisms differ. The highest-error targets identified by the local audit occur after reported recovery, which **weakens contamination as an explanation for the dominant residual** and supports a genuinely extreme CME forecasting problem. Unknown: exact independent corroboration for each hourly row, any contribution from earlier compromised rows, current irreducible forecast error, and whether any causal observation set can reach S<=48.74622.

The most decision-changing next observation is an independently calibrated, versioned speed series over January19–20 at a demonstrably aligned physical location/time, together with the issue-time information actually available to each forecast origin. Preserve the official benchmark unchanged and propose any external blinded/future evaluation as a separately approved protocol.
